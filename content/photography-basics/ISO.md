# What is **ISO?**

![Project Screenshot](ISO.jpg)`

**ISO** controls how sensitive the sensor is to available light. ISO values typically start at 100 and can range all the way to 128,000 depending on the camera. When increasing ISO the sensor starts to create what is called digital noise. Higher the ISO value the more "noisy" you image will turn out. The lower the ISO the cleaner your image will be. Digital noise can be described as a "grainy" effect on your images. While the image is still usable High ISO will reduce image quality. 

# Low **ISO**
## **ISO** 100-200

Generally Provides:
- Cleaner images
- Less digital noise
- Better image quality

# High **ISO**
## ISO 1600+ 

Useful when there is not enough available light.

Increasing **ISO** will introduce noise/grain and reduce image quality.

### Practical Rule

When possible use the lowest **ISO** that allows you to achieve the shutter speed and aperture that you need.
