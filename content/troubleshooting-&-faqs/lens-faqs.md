# Lens FAQs

## What is focal length?

Focal length describes the lens's angle of view and magnification.

Generally:

- **Wide-angle lenses** capture more of the scene.
- **Standard lenses** provide a natural-looking field of view.
- **Telephoto lenses** provide a narrower field of view and magnify distant subjects.

---

## What is the difference between a prime lens and a zoom lens?

A **prime lens** has one fixed focal length.

A **zoom lens** can cover multiple focal lengths.

### Prime Lens

Advantages:

- Often has a wide maximum aperture
- Can be lightweight
- Simple composition
- Good for low-light photography

### Zoom Lens

Advantages:

- Multiple focal lengths
- Flexible composition
- Convenient for travel
- Reduces the need to change lenses

---

## What lens is best for portraits?

Common portrait focal lengths include:

- 50mm
- 85mm
- 105mm
- 135mm

An aperture such as **f/1.8 or f/2.8** can help create background blur and separate the subject from the background.

---

## What lens should I use for landscapes?

Wide-angle lenses are commonly used for landscapes.

Examples include:

- 14mm
- 16mm
- 20mm
- 24mm
- 16–35mm zoom

The best focal length depends on the scene and the desired composition.

---

## What lens should I use for wildlife?

Wildlife photographers often use telephoto or super-telephoto lenses.

Examples include:

- 100–400mm
- 200–500mm
- 400mm
- 600mm

Longer focal lengths allow photographers to photograph subjects from greater distances.

---

## How do I know if a lens is compatible with my camera?

Check:

1. Camera brand
2. Lens mount
3. Camera sensor size
4. Autofocus compatibility
5. Image stabilization compatibility
6. Whether an adapter is required

The lens mount is especially important because different camera systems use different mounts
