# Camera Settings FAQs

## What is the exposure triangle?

The exposure triangle describes the relationship between:

- **Aperture**
- **Shutter speed**
- **ISO**

These three settings affect the brightness of a photograph and can also affect depth of field, motion blur, and image noise.

---

## What does aperture do?

Aperture controls the size of the opening inside the lens.

A **wide aperture**, such as f/1.8, allows more light into the camera and can create a shallow depth of field.

A **narrow aperture**, such as f/11, allows less light into the camera and can keep more of the scene in focus.

---

## What does shutter speed do?

Shutter speed controls how long the camera's shutter remains open.

### Fast shutter speeds

Examples:

- 1/500
- 1/1000
- 1/2000

Useful for freezing motion.

### Slow shutter speeds

Examples:

- 1/30
- 1 second
- 10 seconds

Useful for:

- Low-light photography
- Light trails
- Long exposures
- Motion blur

---

## What does ISO do?

ISO controls the camera sensor's sensitivity to light.

Higher ISO settings can help when there is not enough light but may increase digital noise.

For example:

- ISO 100–200 → Bright conditions
- ISO 400–800 → Moderate light
- ISO 1600+ → Low-light situations

These are general starting points rather than strict rules.

---

## Why are my photographs blurry?

Common causes include:

- Shutter speed is too slow
- Subject is moving
- Camera movement
- Incorrect focus
- Autofocus problem
- Low-light conditions

Try increasing the shutter speed and checking your focus point.

---

## Why are my photographs too dark?

Possible causes include:

- Shutter speed is too fast
- Aperture is too narrow
- ISO is too low
- Not enough available light

You can try:

- Opening the aperture
- Slowing the shutter speed
- Increasing ISO
- Adding more light

---

## Why are my photographs too bright?

Possible causes include:

- Shutter speed is too slow
- Aperture is too wide
- ISO is too high
- Too much light

Try lowering the ISO, using a faster shutter speed, or using a narrower aperture.
