# Photography Lighting Cheat Sheet

## Overview

Lighting is one of the most important parts of photography. The direction, quality, color, and intensity of light can change how a subject looks and how an image feels.

This cheat sheet provides quick references for choosing lighting settings, understanding light direction, and solving common lighting problems.

---

# Light Direction Cheat Sheet

|Lighting Direction|What It Does|Common Uses|
|---|---|---|
|**Front Light**|Illuminates the subject evenly|Portraits, product photography|
|**Side Light**|Creates shadows and emphasizes texture|Portraits, landscapes, artistic photography|
|**Backlight**|Places light behind the subject|Silhouettes, portraits, creative photography|
|**Top Light**|Creates shadows underneath facial features|Dramatic photography|
|**Bottom Light**|Lights the subject from below|Creative or dramatic effects|
|**45° Light**|Creates balanced highlights and shadows|Portraits, studio photography|

# Hard vs. Soft Light

## Hard Light

Hard light creates strong shadows and high contrast.

**Common sources:**

- Direct sunlight
- Small flash
- Bare light bulb
- Small LED light

**Best for:**

- Dramatic portraits
- Sports photograhy
- Strong textures
- Creative photography

## Soft Light

Soft light creates gradual shadows and lower contrast.

**Common sources:**

- Overcast skies
- Large windows
- Softboxes
- Umbrellas
- Diffused sunlight

**Best for:**

- Portraits
- Weddings
- Product photography
- Food photography
- Natural-looking images

### Quick Tip

**Large light source + close to subject = softer light**

**Small light source + far from subject = harder light**

# Natural Lighting Cheat Sheet

|Lighting Condition|Result|Suggested Approach|
|---|---|---|
|**Sunny**|Bright, hard shadows|Use shade or reflectors|
|**Overcast**|Soft, even light|Great for portraits|
|**Golden Hour**|Warm, soft light|Use for portraits and landscapes|
|**Blue Hour**|Cool, low light|Use a tripod and slower shutter speed|
|**Midday Sun**|Harsh overhead light|Use shade or change the shooting angle|
|**Window Light**|Soft directional light|Place subject near the window|

---

# Golden Hour

Golden hour occurs around sunrise and sunset when the sun is low in the sky.

### Characteristics

- Warm colors
- Soft light
- Long shadows
- Lower contrast
- Pleasant highlights

### Good For

- Portraits
- Landscapes
- Travel photography
- Wedding photography
- Outdoor photography

---

# Blue Hour

Blue hour occurs around twilight before sunrise or after sunset.

### Characteristics

- Cool blue tones
- Lower light levels
- Soft ambient light
- Dramatic skies

### Good For

- City photography
- Landscapes
- Architecture
- Night photography
- Long exposures

# Color Temperature Cheat Sheet

|Light Source|Approximate Color|
|---|---|
|Candlelight|Very warm|
|Tungsten bulb|Warm/orange|
|Golden hour|Warm|
|Daylight|Neutral|
|Overcast sky|Slightly cool|
|Shade|Cool/blue|
|Blue hour|Very cool/blue|

### White Balance Tip

If your photograph looks:

- **Too orange** → Cool the white balance
- **Too blue** → Warm the white balance
- **Too green** → Add magenta
- **Too magenta** → Add green



## Key Light

The main source of light.

**Purpose:** Controls the primary brightness and direction of the subject.

## Fill Light

A secondary light that reduces shadows created by the key light.

**Purpose:** Creates a more balanced appearance.

## Back Light

A light placed behind the subject.

**Purpose:** Separates the subject from the background.
