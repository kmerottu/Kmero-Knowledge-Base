# Camera FAQs

## What is the difference between a DSLR and a mirrorless camera?

A **DSLR** uses a mirror and optical viewfinder to show the photographer the scene through the lens. A **mirrorless camera**does not use a mirror and typically uses an electronic viewfinder or the camera's screen.

Both types of cameras can produce high-quality photographs. The main differences are their design, size, viewfinder technology, and available features.

---

## What camera should a beginner buy?

There is no single camera that is best for every beginner. A beginner should consider:

- Budget
- Camera size and weight
- Photography interests
- Lens availability
- Ease of use
- Autofocus performance
- Video features

A camera that is comfortable to use and fits the photographer's needs is often a good starting point.

---

## How many megapixels do I need?

Megapixels describe the number of pixels captured by a camera.

More megapixels can provide:

- More detail
- More flexibility for cropping
- Larger print sizes

However, megapixels are not the only factor that determines image quality. Sensor size, lens quality, lighting, autofocus, and camera settings are also important.

---

## What is a camera sensor?

A camera sensor is the component that captures light and converts it into digital image information.

Common sensor sizes include:

- Full frame
- APS-C
- Micro Four Thirds
- Medium format

Larger sensors can provide advantages in areas such as low-light performance and depth of field, although the overall camera and lens system also affects results.


---



## What memory card should I use?

Choose a memory card that is compatible with the camera and has enough:

- Storage capacity
    
- Write speed
    
- Read speed
    

Photographers who shoot high-resolution photos or video may need faster and larger-capacity cards.

---

## Do I need extra batteries?

An extra battery can be useful for:

- Long photo sessions
    
- Travel
    
- Events
    
- Weddings
    
- Wildlife photography
    
- Video recording
    

Having a backup battery can prevent a session from ending because the main battery runs out.

---






## Should I use manual mode?

Manual mode gives the photographer complete control over aperture, shutter speed, and ISO.

However, beginners do not have to start with manual mode. Aperture Priority and Shutter Priority can help photographers learn individual settings while the camera manages other exposure adjustments.

# Quick FAQ Reference

|Question|Quick Answer|
|---|---|
|DSLR or mirrorless?|Both can produce high-quality images; compare features and workflow|
|Best portrait lens?|50–135mm is commonly useful|
|Best landscape lens?|Wide-angle lenses are commonly used|
|Best wildlife lens?|Telephoto lenses|
|RAW or JPEG?|RAW provides more editing flexibility|
|What controls exposure?|Aperture, shutter speed, and ISO|
|What freezes motion?|Fast shutter speed|
|What creates background blur?|Wide aperture, longer focal length, and subject/background separation|
|What creates soft light?|Large or diffused light source|
|Do I need a tripod?|Useful for stability and long exposures|
|Why are photos blurry?|Often slow shutter speed, movement, or focus problems|
|Why are photos noisy?|Often high ISO or underexposure|
|Why are photos too dark?|Not enough exposure or light|
|Why are photos too bright?|Too much exposure or light|

# Summary

Photography involves many different pieces of equipment, settings, lighting techniques, and editing tools. Understanding common questions about these topics can help photographers troubleshoot problems and make better decisions.

The most important concepts to understand include **exposure, aperture, shutter speed, ISO, focal length, lighting, camera compatibility, editing, and proper equipment care**. As photographers gain experience, they can use these concepts together to create photographs that match their creative goals
