## Editing Workflow

A consistent editing workflow can make post-processing faster and more organized.

### Basic Workflow

1. **Import photographs**

2. **Organize and review images**

3. **Select the best photographs**

4. **Correct exposure**

5. **Adjust white balance**

6. **Adjust highlights and shadows**

7. **Improve contrast**

8. **Adjust color**

9. **Crop and straighten**

10. **Apply lens corrections**

11. **Use local adjustments**

12. **Reduce noise if necessary**

13. **Sharpen the image**

14. **Review the final image**

15. **Export the photograph**

## Exporting Photographs

After editing is complete, the photograph usually needs to be exported into a format that is appropriate for its intended use.

### JPEG

JPEG is commonly used for:

- Websites
- Social media
- Email
- General sharing

### PNG

PNG is useful when an image requires high-quality graphics or transparency.

### TIFF

TIFF can be used for high-quality images and professional printing workflows.

### Export Settings

Important export settings can include:

- File format
- Image dimensions
- Resolution
- Quality
- Color space
- File size

Photographers should choose export settings based on where the photograph will be used.

## Editing Software

There are many programs available for photography editing.

Common examples include:

- Adobe Lightroom
- Adobe Photoshop
- Capture One
- Affinity Photo
- GIMP
- Darktable

Some programs are designed primarily for organizing and developing photographs, while others provide more advanced image manipulation and retouching tools.
